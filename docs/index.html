A device-agnostic parallel-bar method

Four constraint maps point to the highest-accuracy operating region.

Every eNVM device follows a different route, but the same four constraint models—device, array, sensing, and conversion—must converge before a parallel-bar fabric can reach its highest useful SNDR.

Reusable technique

Analyze any parallel-bar device in the same order.

The method separates universal steps from device-specific numerical parameters, so a new memory technology changes the inputs—not the reasoning workflow.

1

Parameterize the device

Insert its on/off conductance, conductance contrast, state variation, read voltage, and energy assumptions.

2

Solve the local limit

Propagate array, sensing, and ADC non-idealities until the total current-distribution SNDR is known.

3

Scale without hiding error

Keep each local bank near its useful dimension, then map large workloads across banks and reduce digitally.

Device families

Different devices change the route, not the four coordinates.

These are example regimes rather than an exhaustive device list. The same technique also applies to a custom eNVM characterized by its measured conductance and noise behavior.

MRAM

High-conductance regime

Give extra attention to conductance contrast, wire parasitics, and the achievable current range.

ReRAM

Balanced-conductance regime

Evaluate variation and array scaling together; lower conductance can preserve useful separation.

FeFET

High-contrast regime

High contrast helps, but sensing and conversion eventually set the remaining SNDR ceiling.

Custom eNVM

Measured-device regime

Replace the device block with measured conductance, variation, energy, and readout parameters.
