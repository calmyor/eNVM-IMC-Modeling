A device-agnostic parallel-bar method

Four constraint maps point to the highest-accuracy operating region.

Every eNVM device follows a different route, but the same four constraint models govern each one. Device, array, sensing, and conversion must converge before a parallel-bar fabric reaches its highest useful SNDR.

Reusable technique

Analyze any parallel-bar device in the same order.

The method separates universal steps from device-specific numerical parameters. A new memory technology changes the inputs, not the reasoning.

1

Parameterize the device

Insert its on/off conductance, conductance contrast, state variation, read voltage, and energy assumptions.

2

Solve the local limit

Propagate array, sensing, and ADC non-idealities until the total current-distribution SNDR is known.

3

Scale without hiding error

Keep each local bank near its useful dimension, then map large workloads across banks and reduce digitally.

Device families

Different devices change the route, not the four coordinates.

These are example regimes, not an exhaustive list. The same technique applies to any custom eNVM once its conductance and noise behavior are measured.

MRAM

High-conductance regime

Conductance contrast, wire parasitics, and the achievable current range need the most attention.

ReRAM

Balanced-conductance regime

Evaluate variation and array scaling together, since lower conductance can preserve useful separation.

FeFET

High-contrast regime

High contrast helps. However, sensing and conversion eventually set the SNDR ceiling.

Custom eNVM

Measured-device regime

Replace the device block with measured conductance, variation, energy, and readout parameters.

A measured case study of the general method

The JxCDC paper applies the technique to MRAM, ReRAM, and FeFET, validates the behavioral model against a 22 nm MRAM prototype, and maps the resulting SNDR to ResNet-20 accuracy.
